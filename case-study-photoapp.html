Spencer's Retailer — Photo Submission App

Spencer's Corp LLC is a North American retailer with over 600 stores in the U.S. and Canada. It specializes in novelty and gag gifts, clothing, band merchandise, and more. The company also owns the seasonal retailer Spirit Halloween.

The Photo Submission app streamlines the process of reviewing and approving storefront photos for new gift shop openings in a mall — replacing a fragmented, email-based workflow with a structured multi-role approval system across iOS, Android, and dashboard.

Discover

The Challenge

What was the problem?

Before the Photo Submission app, setting up new gift shops in a mall involved a complicated manual process. Vendors emailed storefront photos that were then manually tracked by Admin users and forwarded through several approval stages — DSM users, Zone Managers, and HO users. This process was slow, error-prone, and caused frequent delays in store openings.

What are we solving?

With the Photo Submission app, the workflow is fully digitized. Admin users create and manage photo slots directly in the app. Vendors upload storefront photos, which flow through structured review by DSM users, Zone Managers, and HO users — with automated notifications and status tracking at every step.

Business Goal

Streamline photo management and approval for new retail store setups — improving turnaround times, increasing vendor satisfaction, supporting timely store openings, and ultimately contributing to revenue growth and a stronger market presence.

  • Reduce delays caused by manual email-based photo submission
  • Provide clear role-based visibility into submission and approval status
  • Enable faster store openings through automated approval routing
  • Support vendors with guided, compliant photo upload experiences

Design decisions were informed by stakeholder discussions with product and engineering teams, requirement documentation, security guidelines, and feedback from internal reviews. This approach helped align UX closely with real system behavior and enterprise needs.

Design

End to End full journey

Admin — Slot Creation & Vendor Assignment

Admin sets up inspection slots and assigns them to the appropriate vendor to begin the process.

  • Create slot — admin defines a new inspection slot with relevant details
  • Assign to vendor — slot is assigned to the designated vendor for action
  • Notification sent — vendor receives an email alert with assignment details

Vendor — Requirement Upload & Photo Submission

Vendor receives the assignment notification and uploads all required documents and store photos for DSM review.

  • Receive email notification — vendor is alerted of their assigned slot
  • Upload requirement details — fills in all necessary field information
  • Upload store photos — submits current store images for visual comparison review

DSM — District Manager Photo Review & Comparison

District Manager compares old and new store photos side by side, marks items as not applicable where photos are unavailable, then approves for escalation.

  • Review submission — DSM accesses the vendor's uploaded details and photos
  • Compare photos — side-by-side comparison of previous and new store images
  • Mark not applicable — if photos are unavailable, DSM can flag items as N/A
  • Approve & escalate — once satisfied, DSM approves the review for Zone Manager

ZM — Zone Manager Approval & Home Office Handoff

Zone Manager receives a notification after DSM approval, reviews the submission, and can either reject it back or approve and escalate to Home Office for final review.

  • Receive notification — ZM is alerted once DSM has approved the review
  • Review submission — ZM evaluates DSM's findings and vendor data
  • Reject or approve — ZM can send back for revision or approve to move forward
  • Notify Home Office — on approval, Home Office receives notification for final review

Home Office — Final Review & Setup

Home Office receives the fully reviewed submission from DSM and ZM, conducts a final evaluation, and completes the setup to close the process.

  • Receive notification — HO is alerted after ZM approval
  • Review DSM & ZM inputs — examines all prior review stages and photo comparisons
  • Conduct final review — HO makes the definitive assessment on the submission
  • Complete setup — finalizes configuration and closes the workflow
Impact

Impact

The Photo Submission app replaced a fragmented, email-driven workflow with a structured, role-based digital system — significantly reducing delays and improving visibility across every stage of the storefront approval process.

Measurable Results:

  • Eliminated manual email tracking — all photo submissions, reviews, and approvals now happen in one place across Admin, Vendor, DSM, ZM, and Home Office roles
  • Faster store openings — automated approval routing and real-time notifications reduced back-and-forth delays between roles
  • 5 roles unified into a single workflow — Admin, Vendor, DSM, Zone Manager, and Home Office operate from a shared system with clear role-based views
  • Reduced submission errors — guided photo upload with slot-based structure helped vendors submit compliant images on the first attempt
  • Cross-platform reach — designed for iOS, Android, and dashboard, ensuring every user could access the workflow on their preferred device

Reflections

Conclusion & Learnings

The Spencer’s Photo Submission app set out to solve a simple but costly problem — a slow, fragmented storefront approval process held together by emails. By digitizing the end-to-end workflow and giving each role a purpose-built experience, we turned a painful manual process into a streamlined, trackable system that directly supports faster store openings.

Working as the solo designer across a complex multi-role product in just 8 weeks pushed me to make fast, confident decisions while staying closely aligned with the PM, architect, and engineers throughout. The project reinforced how much clarity of role and flow matters when designing systems where every handoff is critical.

Key Learnings

  • Multi-role systems require deep role empathy — each user (Admin, Vendor, DSM, ZM, HO) had different goals and pain points; designing for all five meant truly understanding each perspective before touching the UI
  • Workflow clarity reduces errors — structuring photo slots with clear requirements upfront dramatically reduced vendor resubmissions and approval back-and-forth
  • Mobile-first thinking paid off — starting with paper wireframes on small screens helped surface navigation and hierarchy issues early, before high-fidelity investment
  • Automated notifications are a UX feature — designing the notification triggers as part of the workflow (not an afterthought) was key to keeping all five roles in sync without manual follow-up
  • Stakeholder alignment shortens design cycles — early discussions with product and engineering ensured that every design decision was buildable within the 8-week timeline
